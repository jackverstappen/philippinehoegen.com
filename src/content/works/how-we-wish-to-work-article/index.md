# Research Article

**How We Wish to Work**

## *An exchange on participation, connectivity and care*

[Philippine Hoegen](https://www.tandfonline.com/author/Hoegen%2C+Philippine) & [Veerle Spronck](https://www.tandfonline.com/author/Spronck%2C+Veerle)

Pages 82-87 \| Published online: 14 Feb 2025

## **Abstract**

In ‘How We Wish to Work’, Philippine Hoegen and Veerle Spronck engage in a dialogue on the intertwining of care and connectivity, and ethics and aesthetics in artistic and research practices. The discussion is on how participatory practices challenge traditional notions of art, authorship and audience engagement. Hoegen reflects on her artistic practice, in which co-creation takes a central place, so that participants become collaborators and co-authors. Spronck complements this by sharing her research into audience participation in symphonic music, highlighting the implicit norms and complexities of collaborative involvement.

The conversation broadens to the ethics of care. Drawing on thinkers like Joan Tronto and Carol Gilligan, the authors position care as a networked, relational activity fundamental to collective and participatory work. They explore the intersection of care and connectivity, concluding that ethical and participative art practices necessitate rethinking roles, power dynamics, and shared values within the creative process.

Ultimately, this article suggests that the aesthetic and ethical dimensions of artistic practices are inseparable, with the meaning of the work lying as much in the nature of the collective process as in the artistic concepts. Hoegen and Spronck propose that connective artistic work, far from being a mere act of inclusion, is both a means and an end, because it is the site of rehearsing ethics, relationality and collectivity, ultimately contributing to new and necessary social and relational imaginaries. Ethics and aesthetics, they propose, can therefore not be disconnected.

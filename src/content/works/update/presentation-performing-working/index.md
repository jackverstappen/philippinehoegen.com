Introduction to the Professional Doctorate by Philippine Hoegen for the HKU, Performing Working:

What is work, what is not work, how to work without exhausting yourself and/or your environment? Who are we when we work, and who if we are not able to work? Why do we give so much importance to work in our lives? What makes that certain activities or roles are referred to as work and what happens when the term is applied to activities that are not normally called work, but which include many or all of the elements often applied to categorise something as work? These are the urgent questions people are grappling with in the many exchanges that are taking place in the preparation of this research, and the answers people themselves give are paradoxical. This research aims to contribute to a better understanding of what work is in our lives, and of what it demands from us and what we should demand of it

The doctoral project, *Performing Working*, approaches work through the lens of performance and performance through the lens of work. The project investigates who we are when we work—and who we become when we cannot, do not, or are structurally excluded from work. Hoegen critically examines how waged labour is idealised as the primary measure of participation and citizenship, and how this privileging produces social exclusion for many people, including those living with illness or disability. She explores undervalued, hidden, and unwaged forms of labour and the socially ingrained mechanisms that render them invisible.

*Performing Working* is part of a new, practice-led professional doctorate launched in 2023 within Dutch Universities of Applied Sciences, equivalent in level to a PhD but designed specifically for artistic and professional research. Documentation and materials related to the project, as well as Hoegen’s broader artistic portfolio, can be found on the Research Catalogue.
